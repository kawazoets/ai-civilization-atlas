## The Cleaner Who Collapsed and the Indian Workers Who Immediately Stepped In

Working at a semiconductor manufacturing site in Singapore, I sometimes encounter moments that leave a strong impression on me even though they have nothing to do with equipment or construction work.

One day, an elderly cleaner who regularly cleans the vendor area collapsed right in front of me while he was working. Just before he fell, I saw him trying to sit down on a chair and support himself with his hands, but he could no longer hold himself up and sank to the floor.

I immediately called out to the people around me to coordinate assistance and ran to the office floor on the third level.

What stayed with me, however, was the way the Indian workers around him responded, and I want to record that here.

Without anyone telling them what to do, some of them cleared the area so that the man could lie down. Someone offered him water from his own bottle. Several workers naturally stepped in and tried to help him.

What struck me most was how natural and ordinary their response seemed.

Later, I spoke about what had happened with a representative from the company that manages the Indian workers.

I asked him why they were able to respond so quickly in a situation like that.

He told me that perhaps it was because they often see their fellow workers in situations where someone needs help.

Those words stayed with me as well.

The experience also made me reflect on my own actions.

After the cleaner collapsed, I took it upon myself to go to the office floor and report what had happened. Of course, I believe that was something that needed to be done at the time. But unlike those workers, it did not come naturally to me to immediately offer my own water or make space for the man to lie down.

I felt a little ashamed of that.

What I still find myself wondering is:

**“Why were they able to respond so naturally in that moment?”**

Does kindness come simply from a person's character?

Or does it come from repeatedly seeing people in difficult situations and from experiences of helping one another, until eventually the body begins to act before the mind has time to think?

I still don't know.

But that day, at a semiconductor factory, I observed something that had nothing to do with equipment. I observed something about people. And at the same time, I observed something about myself. I want to keep a record of that experience.
