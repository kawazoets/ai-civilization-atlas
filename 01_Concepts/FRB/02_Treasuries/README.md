# 02. Treasuries

## Why Does the Federal Reserve Hold So Many U.S. Treasuries?

When most people hear the word "Treasuries," they think of government debt.

However, from the Federal Reserve's perspective, U.S. Treasuries are much more than government bonds.

They are one of the primary tools used to manage liquidity, interest rates, and confidence in the financial system.

Understanding why the Federal Reserve holds such a large amount of Treasuries is essential for understanding modern monetary policy.

---

# What Are U.S. Treasuries?

Treasuries are debt securities issued by the U.S. Treasury to finance government spending.

When investors purchase Treasuries, they are effectively lending money to the U.S. government in exchange for future repayment with interest.

Examples include:

- Treasury Bills (T-Bills)
- Treasury Notes
- Treasury Bonds

These securities are generally considered among the safest financial assets in the world because they are backed by the credit of the U.S. government.

---

# Why Doesn't the FRB Simply Print Money?

This is one of the most common questions.

If the Federal Reserve can create dollars, why does it bother buying Treasury securities?

The answer lies in accounting.

Every liability must be supported by an asset.

If the Federal Reserve creates new dollars, something must appear on the asset side of its balance sheet.

Treasuries serve as that asset.

Rather than creating money from nothing, the Federal Reserve exchanges newly created reserves for Treasury securities.

---

# How Treasury Purchases Create Liquidity

Suppose a commercial bank owns $100 million of U.S. Treasuries.

The Federal Reserve purchases those securities.

The balance sheets change as follows.

Commercial Bank

```text
Assets

-100M Treasuries
+100M Bank Reserves
```

Federal Reserve

```text
Assets

+100M Treasuries

----------------------------

Liabilities

+100M Bank Reserves
```

No physical cash is printed.

Instead, reserve balances increase.

This process injects liquidity into the banking system.

---

# Treasuries Are Not an Investment

An important point is that the Federal Reserve does not buy Treasuries because it wants investment returns.

Its objective is monetary policy.

Treasuries are used because they are:

- Highly liquid
- Extremely low credit risk
- Traded in deep global markets
- Suitable for large-scale operations

For the Federal Reserve, Treasuries function as monetary instruments rather than investment assets.

---

# Why Treasuries Matter to Interest Rates

Treasury yields influence borrowing costs throughout the economy.

Examples include:

- Mortgage rates
- Corporate bond yields
- Auto loans
- Student loans

When the Federal Reserve buys Treasuries, demand increases.

Higher demand generally pushes Treasury prices up and yields down.

Lower Treasury yields often lead to lower borrowing costs across the financial system.

This is one of the transmission mechanisms of monetary policy.

---

# Connection to Quantitative Easing (QE)

During periods of economic stress, the Federal Reserve purchases large quantities of Treasuries.

This policy is known as Quantitative Easing (QE).

The objectives include:

- Increasing liquidity
- Lowering long-term interest rates
- Supporting financial markets
- Encouraging lending and investment

The purchases expand both sides of the Federal Reserve's balance sheet.

---

# Connection to FIMA Repo

Treasuries also play another important role.

Foreign central banks, including the Bank of Japan, hold large amounts of U.S. Treasuries.

Under the FIMA Repo Facility, these Treasuries can be temporarily pledged as collateral in exchange for U.S. dollars.

This allows foreign central banks to obtain dollar liquidity without selling their Treasury holdings.

The same securities therefore serve two functions:

- Monetary policy inside the United States.
- Dollar liquidity outside the United States.

---

# Key Takeaways

Treasuries are much more than government debt.

For the Federal Reserve, they are the foundation of modern monetary operations.

The Federal Reserve does not hold Treasuries to earn interest income.

Instead, Treasuries provide the accounting foundation that allows the Federal Reserve to supply liquidity, influence interest rates, and support global dollar funding markets.

---

# Learning Note

Today's study changed my understanding of U.S. Treasuries.

Previously, I viewed them primarily as government debt.

Now I see them as one of the fundamental operating tools of the Federal Reserve.

Treasuries are not simply investments.

They are assets that support the creation of central bank liabilities, influence interest rates through market operations, and serve as collateral for international dollar liquidity facilities such as FIMA Repo.

Understanding Treasuries is therefore not simply about government finance.

It is about understanding how capital, liquidity, and confidence are managed throughout the global financial system.
