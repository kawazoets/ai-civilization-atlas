# AI Capitalism — From Research Papers to a Book, and Toward the Next Update

## August 2026

I published the English and Japanese editions of *AI Capitalism* on Amazon Kindle.

But for me, this publication does not mean that I have finished writing a book.

Rather, I see it as the point at which the research, observations, hypotheses, and papers I have developed over time have been integrated, for the first time, into the form of a book.

In other words, this is not the completion of the project.

**This is AI Capitalism Version 1.0.**

---

## This Book Did Not Begin as a Book

The idea of AI Capitalism was not originally created with the intention of writing a book.

How will AI change the way companies are valued?

What happens when AI, rather than humans alone, begins to evaluate companies and assets?

What kind of structure will companies need if they are to become readable by AI?

Where will capital move as AI expands?

What role will physical infrastructure — compute, semiconductors, data centers, electricity, and logistics — play in an AI-driven economy?

I pursued these questions one by one, wrote papers around them, and published the results.

Along the way, themes such as:

* AI-Scored Society
* AI-Readable Enterprise
* AI Valuation
* AI Capitalism
* AI × Gold
* Compute and Capital
* AI Infrastructure and Moving Bottlenecks

gradually began to connect.

I did not begin with one grand theory and then try to prove it.

It happened the other way around.

**By continuing to follow small questions, a larger structure gradually became visible.**

That structure became AI Capitalism.

---

## The Papers Form the Roots of This Book

For that reason, *AI Capitalism* does not exist as a standalone book.

Beneath it lies a body of papers I have written over time.

And beneath those papers lie everyday observations.

In simplified form, the research structure looks like this:

**Real-World Observation**
↓
**Question**
↓
**Hypothesis**
↓
**Paper / Research**
↓
**AI Civilization Atlas**
↓
**AI Capitalism — Book**

But the arrows do not stop there.

Reality continues to move after the book is published.

So after the book comes another cycle:

**New Observation**
↓
**Verification**
↓
**Correction**
↓
**New Hypothesis**
↓
**Revision**

This is therefore not a linear process.

It is a cycle.

---

## Singapore as an Observation Point

While writing this book, I was not in Silicon Valley or on Wall Street.

I was in Singapore.

And I was observing part of the physical infrastructure supporting the AI economy being built in the real world.

Semiconductor manufacturing equipment.

Automated material handling systems.

PLCs.

AMRs.

Logistics.

Electricity.

Factories.

And behind them, compute demand and capital.

When we think about AI, our attention naturally tends to move toward models, algorithms, GPUs, and software.

But when you observe the physical world, AI cannot operate on those things alone.

Factories are required.

Electricity is required.

Equipment is required.

Logistics are required.

People are required.

And capital is required to build all of them.

This connection to the physical world became an important observation point in developing the idea of AI Capitalism.

---

## Publication Is Not the End of the Research

Normally, when a book is published, we consider it “finished.”

But when writing about AI, I think that way of thinking can be somewhat dangerous.

The AI environment is changing extremely quickly.

The cost of compute changes.

Model capabilities change.

Semiconductor supply structures change.

Data centers expand.

Electricity demand changes.

Corporate adoption of AI changes.

The way capital markets value companies changes.

And the range of economic activity that AI itself may observe, evaluate, and make decisions about could continue to expand.

Naturally, some of the hypotheses in *AI Capitalism*, written in 2026, may eventually prove to be wrong.

That is fine.

What matters more is:

**recording where I was wrong.**

---

## AI Capitalism Version 1.0

In that sense, I do not regard the book I have just published as a finished product.

It is:

**AI Capitalism Version 1.0.**

It represents the world I was able to observe as of August 2026.

The information available to me at that point.

The hypotheses I was able to formulate at that point.

My understanding, at that point, of the relationship between AI and capital.

It is a snapshot that fixes those things at a particular moment in time.

As reality changes, AI Capitalism should change as well.

If new evidence emerges, I will add it.

If a hypothesis proves wrong, I will correct it.

If a different structure becomes visible, I will write another paper.

And if necessary, I will eventually update the book itself.

---

## Beyond the Question of Whether It Sells

Of course, having published it as a book, I want people to read it.

And I would be happy if it sells.

But I do not intend to measure the value of this project only by the number of copies sold.

What matters to me is:

**how reality will force this book to change.**

If AI Capitalism contains ideas that are correct, signs of them should eventually appear in the real world.

If some of those ideas are wrong, contradictions should appear in the real world as well.

I want to observe both.

---

## The Role of the Atlas

That is why the AI Civilization Atlas exists.

The Atlas is not a place for proving that my ideas were correct.

Nor is it a place where I preserve only successful predictions.

**It is a place for recording how ideas emerged and how reality subsequently forced them to change.**

A paper fixes a hypothesis at a particular point in time.

A book integrates multiple hypotheses into a larger structure.

And the Atlas records the changes that occur before and after them.

These are not separate activities.

They are different layers of a single research process.

---

## Toward the Next Observation

Publishing *AI Capitalism* does not mean that the research is over.

Rather, it has created a reference point against which future observations can be compared.

**This is how I saw the relationship between AI and capital in August 2026.**

I have now been able to preserve that view.

That makes what I need to observe next much clearer.

What changed?

What did not change?

Which hypotheses survived?

Which hypotheses broke?

And what did I fail to see?

I will continue observing those questions.

Publication is not the finish line.

**It is a new reference point from which the next observation begins.**
