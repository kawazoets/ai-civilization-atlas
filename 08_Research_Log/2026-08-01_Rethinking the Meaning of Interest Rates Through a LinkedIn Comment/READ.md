# Rethinking the Meaning of Interest Rates Through a LinkedIn Comment

Recently, I received my first comment on LinkedIn from an entrepreneur overseas.

He is the founder of a software company in Lithuania, and in his response to one of my posts, he introduced a new perspective through a single phrase:

**Lead Time.**

At first, I thought he was simply talking about AI infrastructure.

But the moment I read his comment from the perspective of **lead time**, what came to my mind was not GPUs or transformers.

It was **cash flow**.

## What Lies Behind the Word "Lead Time"

The term *lead time* is common in both manufacturing and software development.

However, to someone who has run a business, the phrase carries a very different meaning.

A project is delayed.

The invoice cannot be issued.

Payment is delayed.

Meanwhile,

employee salaries,

office rent,

outsourcing costs,

and fixed operating expenses

must still be paid every month.

Whether revenue is high or zero, those expenses do not change.

Before profit even becomes an issue, if cash stops flowing, the company is over.

The feeling of breaking into a cold sweat every month is something only people who have experienced managing cash flow can truly understand.

His comment brought those memories back to me.

## The Same Words, But Different Meanings

Of course, I believe his comment was about lead times across AI infrastructure.

However, I sensed something uniquely familiar behind his words.

This is only my interpretation.

But perhaps because I have also run a business, I instinctively read the monthly pressure hidden behind the phrase **Lead Time**.

Today I work on a manufacturing site, where lead time is simply part of the job.

For a business owner, however, lead time is much more than a schedule.

It determines when revenue can be recognized.

It determines when cash arrives.

In other words, it is part of cash flow itself.

The same words can carry completely different meanings depending on where you stand.

## Why It Brought Me Back to the Question of Interest Rates

For some time, I have been thinking about a simple question:

**What is Interest?**

Most explanations define interest as **the cost of borrowing money**.

For business owners, however, it feels slightly different.

Interest is also **the cost of bridging time**.

The time until revenue arrives.

The time until delivery is completed.

The time until an invoice can be issued.

Businesses raise capital to survive that period.

That is why a single phrase — **Lead Time** — naturally brought me back to a theme I had explored before: **interest rates**.

## Observing AI Infrastructure

As I continue observing AI infrastructure, I notice that the requirements keep expanding.

It is no longer just about GPUs.

Now it includes FC-BGA substrates,

transformers,

electricity,

power grids,

construction,

water,

permits,

and much more.

As the system expands, lead times are becoming longer.

Looking at those lead times, I realized I was no longer simply observing delays in AI infrastructure.

I had begun thinking again about the value of **time** itself.

> Even the same US$10,000 has a completely different meaning.
>
> US$10,000 received today
>
> is not the same as US$10,000 received six months later.

A single phrase — **Lead Time** — brought me back to a question I have been asking for some time:

**What is Interest?**

## Learning Note

What impressed me most was not simply receiving a comment from an entrepreneur on LinkedIn.

It was that a single phrase — **Lead Time** — brought back memories of running my own business and allowed me to rethink the meaning of interest rates from a completely different perspective.

I thought I had been observing AI infrastructure.

Through that comment, however, I realized I had also rediscovered how business owners perceive the value of time.
