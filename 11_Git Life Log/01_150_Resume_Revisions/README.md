# I Have Rewritten My Résumé More Than 150 Times

It has been about ten years since I came to Singapore. Over those ten years, I have rewritten my résumé more than 150 times. I counted the PDFs in the folder where I keep them, and that was the number.

Every time I changed jobs, I updated it. I changed the content depending on the company I was applying to, and whenever I failed to get through a document screening or an interview, I revised it again. I changed not only the Japanese version but also the English wording, rewrote my Professional Summary, and experimented with different ways of presenting my experience.

After repeating this process over and over, the number somehow exceeded 150. This is not mountain climbing, but even now, at the age of 58, I am still rewriting my résumé.

I had been aiming for early retirement since 30 years ago, but life does not always go according to plan.

For the past two years, I have also been using AI. In the past, even revising a single expression could take time. Now I can compare several alternatives in a short period of time. Ironically, this may have led me to rewrite my résumé even more often, because I now pay closer attention to subtle nuances than I did before.

## I Have Not Followed a Single Professional Path

When I look at the jobs I have experienced so far, even to me they seem quite scattered.

Sales.  
Planning.  
Administration.  
Systems.  
Human Resources.  
Accounting.  
Finance.

And now, I work at a semiconductor manufacturing site.

In my current job, I work between Japanese engineers involved in manufacturing equipment and multinational staff, providing interpretation and coordination.

PLC, control panels, AMRs, conveyors — words that once had almost nothing to do with my life are now part of my everyday work.

I have not become an engineer. Nor did I ever set out to become one. Even so, I need to understand what the engineers are talking about and where a particular problem sits within the overall equipment system.

So I have been learning the things I do not understand, one by one.

## AI Changed the Way I Learn

The arrival of generative AI brought a significant change to my life. I do not mean that in either a positive or negative sense. I am simply describing what happened.

In the past, when I encountered an unfamiliar technical term, I would search for it, read several websites, and sometimes give up if I still could not understand it.

Now I can ask AI questions as many times as I need.

I can start with, "What is a PLC?" and then gradually narrow down my questions: "Where is the PLC inside this control panel?" "Why is 24VDC necessary?" "What does I/O do?" I can keep breaking the questions down until I reach a level I can understand.

The same is true for English.

My English did not suddenly become better. But the distance between encountering something I do not understand and being able to check it, revise my own wording, and put it into a form that communicates clearly to someone else has become much shorter.

And when something is needed at the worksite, I have also started creating simple resources such as Quick Help guides myself on GitHub.

AI has not made me an expert. Rather, **it has shortened the distance that someone like me, who is not an expert, has to travel to enter an unfamiliar field.**

## My Career Is Still Not Complete

Reading this far, it might sound like a success story about how "even at 58, you can take on a new kind of work."

But it is not.

I still do not have a permanent position. I am a temporary worker on a fixed-term contract. And I do not yet know what I will do next.

That is why I am rewriting my résumé again.

I cannot pretend to tell other people, "This is how you should work after 50," because I still do not have the answer myself.

There is, however, one thing I know for certain from these ten years. Every time my job changed, I learned, little by little, whatever I needed at the time. Sometimes that was sales. Sometimes it was human resources. Sometimes it was accounting or finance.

And now I am learning about PLCs and AMRs. The same goes for GPUs and AI.

I have not built a career by neatly accumulating expertise in one single field. Instead, whenever circumstances required it, I have rewritten my own role.

Perhaps rewriting my résumé more than 150 times is simply a record of that process.

In interviews, I have sometimes been told that my career lacks consistency or a clear direction. I understand why it can look that way. But this is the life I have lived.

## How Do I Relate to Work in the Age of AI?

Recently, I often see discussions about which jobs AI will eliminate and which jobs will remain.

I myself have written and researched extensively about AI. But when I think about it as a question concerning my own life, I am becoming less concerned with how to keep working in the age of AI. Instead, **I have started thinking about why I work in the age of AI, and what kind of relationship I want to have with work.**

AI will probably become capable of doing even more in the years ahead. Over the past two years, I myself have already come to rely on AI for many more things. Writing in English, learning unfamiliar technologies, researching information, and studying have all become much easier than they were before.

And perhaps precisely because of that, I find myself spending more time thinking not only about what human beings are capable of doing, but also about **what I want to do, and why I want to do it.**

Even at the semiconductor manufacturing site where I work now, I did not enter the job looking for some grand meaning in it. I researched what I did not understand, listened to people, and looked for things I could do. Somewhere along the way, I began to see work a little differently than I had before.

Perhaps work is not only about providing expertise that has already been fully developed. It may also be about entering an unfamiliar place, thinking about what is needed there, and gradually discovering what you can contribute.

At least for me, this is becoming closer to what it means to engage with work.

At 58, I am still looking for the answer. And even as I write this, I am rewriting my résumé again.

More than 150 times. I have written enough.

**If possible, I really do not want to rewrite my résumé anymore.**
